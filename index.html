Even after paying Seated's fee, this profit stays positive

Seated guests bring their own revenue and carry none of your fixed costs, so the profit they add is simply revenue − F&B cost − the Seated fee. That stays positive across nearly any realistic reward rate — it would take an extreme fee, far above what Seated actually charges, before adding these guests ever cost you money.

The simple way to think about it

Every Seated guest is either a sale you wouldn't have had, or a guest you would've served anyway.

    The intuition to remember: Seated's fee doesn't erase your margin — it makes it slightly smaller on the guests you'd have gotten anyway, and pure incremental profit on everyone else. Because the "new guest" gain is so much larger than the "regular guest" cost, each new guest effectively pays for several regulars.

    Where the breakeven line actually is

    As the share of Seated guests who are repeat customers climbs from 0% to 100%, net profit slides from "all new guests" down to "all regulars" — in a straight line. Where it crosses zero is the breakeven rate above.
